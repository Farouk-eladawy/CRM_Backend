# Do Uber and Bolt Work at Egyptian Airports?

Uber and Bolt work well at Cairo International Airport, work patchily at Hurghada and Sharm El Sheikh, and are effectively unavailable at smaller airports like Luxor, Aswan, and Marsa Alam. Both apps are legal and popular in Egypt's big cities, but airport coverage is a different question from city coverage — and getting it wrong leaves you stranded in arrivals with no plan.

## Cairo: apps work, with caveats

At Cairo Airport, both Uber and Bolt are available and widely used. The in-app price removes haggling, you can pay by card, and you can track the car. For a confident traveller it is a reasonable option.

The caveats matter, though. Airport pickup zones are confusing and sometimes a walk from the terminal, drivers can struggle to find you among the crowds, and cancellations are common — especially for long fares like the cross-city run to Giza. At busy times or late at night the wait can stretch out. The app removes the price argument but not the uncertainty of whether a car will actually turn up.

## Hurghada and Sharm El Sheikh: patchy at best

In the Red Sea resort towns, app coverage is thin and inconsistent. You might get a Bolt in central Hurghada, but at the airport the supply of drivers is low and waits can be long or fruitless. Sharm El Sheikh is similar. These are resort economies built around hotel transfers and tour operators, not a dense pool of app drivers circling for fares, so relying on an app at the airport is a gamble.

### Why resort airports are different

Resort airports handle waves of charter and package arrivals, and the transport ecosystem reflects that. Most arriving passengers have a hotel transfer arranged, so there is little incentive for app drivers to wait around. When a flight lands you are competing for a handful of cars, if any are online at all.

## Luxor, Aswan, and Marsa Alam: don't count on apps

At Egypt's smaller airports, ride apps are essentially not an option. Luxor and Aswan have limited or no reliable app coverage, and Marsa Alam, which mainly serves resort traffic far from any city, has none to speak of. Here the realistic choices are a pre-arranged transfer or an airport taxi, and the taxi market at these smaller airports skews heavily toward tourist pricing.

## The reliability gap that catches people out

The core problem with apps at any Egyptian airport is not whether they technically work, but whether you can depend on them at the moment you arrive. A cancelled ride downtown means a five-minute wait for another. A cancelled ride at an airport — tired, late, with luggage, possibly with no second car available — is a genuine problem. The further you are from central Cairo, the worse those odds get.

## When apps make sense and when they don't

If you are landing at Cairo in daylight, travelling light, and happy to wait a little and walk to a pickup point, an app is a fine, cheap choice. If you are arriving late, in a group, with lots of luggage, at a resort or regional airport, or you simply cannot afford for the ride to fall through, an app is the wrong tool. The downside risk is too high for the small saving.

## The bottom line

Uber and Bolt do work at Egyptian airports — but really only at Cairo, and even there with cancellation and pickup-zone headaches. At the Red Sea resorts they are unreliable, and at Luxor, Aswan, and Marsa Alam you should not plan around them at all. The smaller or later your arrival, the more a guaranteed ride matters.
