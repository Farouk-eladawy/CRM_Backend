# Is It Safe to Take a Taxi from Cairo Airport Alone?

Yes, it is generally safe to take a taxi from Cairo Airport alone. Violent crime against tourists is rare, and thousands of solo travellers arrive at Cairo International every day without incident. The real risks are not physical danger but overcharging, confusion, and the occasional driver who takes a longer route or pressures you toward a shop or hotel he is paid to recommend.

## What actually goes wrong at the airport

The most common problem solo arrivals face is the price. Cairo airport taxis rarely use a meter for tourists, and a fare that should be a few hundred Egyptian pounds can quietly become several times that if you negotiate badly or look unsure. Touts inside the arrivals hall add to the pressure. Men will approach you offering "official" taxis, help with bags, or a "special price" — many are freelancers, not airport staff, and the price climbs once your luggage is in the boot.

Late-night arrivals feel the most exposed. If your flight lands at 2am, you are tired, the hall is quieter, and you have less leverage to walk away from a bad deal. This is when most overcharging happens.

## How to keep yourself safe and avoid being overcharged

A few simple habits make a solo arrival far smoother. Agree on the full price before you get in, in writing or by showing the number on your phone. Keep your bags with you until the fare is settled. Sit in the back, and have small notes ready so you are not handing over a large bill and hoping for change.

Trust your instinct over politeness. If a driver insists on a detour, a "better hotel," or a stop you did not ask for, it is completely normal to say no firmly. You are not being rude — you are doing what locals do every day.

### For women travelling alone

Egypt is more conservative than many home countries, and solo women can attract extra attention or unwanted conversation. It is rarely threatening, but it can be tiring after a long flight. Sitting in the back seat, keeping interactions brief, and having a clearly arranged ride waiting all reduce the friction. Many women prefer a pre-arranged driver precisely so the first face they see is one holding their name on a sign, not a crowd of touts.

## Why a metered city taxi still leaves gaps

Egypt's white metered taxis and ride apps work well inside the city, but the airport is a special case. Drivers know arrivals don't know the going rate, the meter is often "broken," and the queue outside terminals can be chaotic at peak times. Even seasoned travellers who happily flag taxis downtown tend to find the airport the one place where the odds are stacked against a newcomer.

## The bottom line

Taking a taxi from Cairo Airport alone is safe in the sense that matters most: you are very unlikely to come to harm. The genuine risks are financial and logistical, and they are easiest to face when you have planned ahead. Stay alert, settle the price first, and trust your gut, and a solo arrival in Cairo is perfectly manageable.
