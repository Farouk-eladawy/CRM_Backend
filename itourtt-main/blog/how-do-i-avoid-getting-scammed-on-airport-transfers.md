# How Do I Avoid Getting Scammed on Airport Transfers?

The way to avoid getting scammed on an Egyptian airport transfer is to settle the price and the vehicle before you arrive, never let your bags out of reach until the fare is agreed, and ignore anyone who approaches you unprompted in arrivals. Airport transfer scams in Egypt are almost always about money, not danger — overcharging, fake "official" taxis, and small confidence tricks — and a few habits defuse nearly all of them.

## The common scams to know

The classic airport trick is the inflated fare. A driver quotes a price several times the real rate, banking on you not knowing better. Close behind is the "broken meter," which means the price becomes whatever is negotiated, always in the driver's favour. Then there are the touts: men inside arrivals who claim to be official taxi staff, offer to carry your bags, or steer you to a "special" car — and the price climbs once your luggage is loaded.

Two more catch people regularly. The "no change" trick turns a large note into a rounded-up fare. And the unrequested detour — to a papyrus shop, a perfume "factory," or a "better hotel" — exists because the driver earns a commission for delivering you there.

### The "your hotel is closed" trick

One scam deserves its own warning: a driver who claims your hotel is closed, overbooked, or "not good," and offers to take you somewhere better. It is almost always false and aimed at a commission. Insist on going to the hotel you booked. If you have any doubt, call the hotel directly to confirm — a real hotel will happily reassure you.

## The warning signs

A few signals reliably mark a ride you should walk away from. A driver who will not give a clear total before you set off. A meter that is suddenly broken. Pressure to decide quickly or to follow someone away from the official rank. An eagerness to load your bags before any price is agreed. And any unrequested stop or change of destination. None of these are normal for an honest ride, and each is a cue to step back.

## How to protect yourself on the spot

The defensive habits are simple and they work. Agree the full price before the wheels move, and keep your bags with you until you have. Carry small notes so "no change" cannot inflate the fare. Decline help you did not ask for, politely but firmly. And never feel obliged to follow someone who approached you — at a busy airport there is always another car, and walking away is your strongest move.

If a quote feels wrong, it usually is. You are allowed to say no, repeat your destination, and refuse any detour without explanation.

## The bottom line

You avoid airport transfer scams in Egypt by denying them the conditions they need: agree the price first, guard your bags, carry small notes, refuse unrequested help and detours, and never trust the "your hotel is closed" line. Better still, arrive with the ride already booked, so a known driver is waiting and there is nothing to negotiate at all.
